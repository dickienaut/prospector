# Prospector as a product — the tunable pattern-finder, and detecting the theme before it packages

**What this is:** the two captured what-if notes — *the tunable pattern-finder* (2026-07-03) and *detecting the theme before it packages / proto-cluster* (2026-07-04) — combined into **one source of truth** (this supersedes both; archive the originals). It is a **dream worth not losing, not a plan**: the product vision underneath Prospector, its sharpest capability (finding a theme before anyone packages it), the two things that would make it real rather than dangerous, and the one number that gates the whole business. Nothing here is started now; it is captured so it is buildable later. Connects to §1.3–§1.9, §2.4, §2.9, §3.2.

---

## 1. The core reframe — a pattern-*finder*, not a matcher

Prospector is a pattern-*finding* machine, not a pattern-*matching* one. A matcher only finds shapes you specified in advance — which is the consensus trap by construction, since you can only match the already-named. A finder detects structure nobody templated yet (Tech×Utilities cohering, Financials decoupling in 2020, a proto-cluster forming) via unsupervised departure-from-baseline.

The consequence that opens the product door: the patterns it finds are **objective-neutral**. The 2020 Financials decoupling was real structure; it was "off-thesis" only relative to *the owner's* emerging-tech objective — to a rates or macro trader it is the signal of the year. So the finder is separable from the objective, which means the objective is a **tunable layer on top of an objective-neutral engine**. Prospector is that engine tuned to one brain (17+ year horizon, emerging tech, over-trading as the enemy). The product would be the same engine, tuned by each user to their own objective.

## 2. Why the niche is empty — the underserved market

Incumbents genuinely *can't* build it. An institution needs pre-consensus alpha on 1–3 year horizons, so "consensus-but-true is fine" is worthless to them; an engagement-monetizing vendor needs the idea-firehose to *be* the product, so "made you act less" is unsellable. But "incumbents won't build it" ≠ "no one would buy it" — that combination is exactly what an **underserved** market looks like. Two tailwinds: offense sells better than defense (nobody dreams of a smoke detector; everybody wants to find the thing early), and the barrier to publishing has collapsed — a static site, trivial hosting, social marketing, a few paying users to cover costs is within reach.

## 3. The flagship capability — detecting the theme before it packages

The sharpest expression of the finder is the proto-cluster / pseudo-ETF direction (§1.9): a thesis no one has packaged and no one is loudly discussing is still visible in the substrate, as a cluster of names co-moving across the existing sector boundaries — a pseudo-ETF you construct yourself, the **earliest possible T0** for the cross-stream lag.

The commercial turn: for the 17-year owner, the disciplined move is to detect at T0 and act late, through the wall, when a clean instrument exists — the horizon (§1.3) makes that timing gap costless, so there is no reason to hold a hand-built single-name basket. But a **product** serves users who are *not* 17-year owners. For a shorter-horizon user who explicitly wants early single-name exposure, "here is the theme forming, months before anyone packages it" is a genuine value proposition, not a discipline violation. The owner's rule ("own industries via rules-based ETFs, never pick winners") is **bracketed** for the product context — true for the owner personally, a user preference not a law of the tool. Once bracketed, the question stops being "should I act early" (settled) and becomes "is the signal real, early, and detectable — enough to sell honestly."

Why it should be there at all, structurally (not wishfully): §1.5's whole premise **requires** it — launches are near-consensus *by construction*, so the underlying names syncing up must predate the packaging almost by definition; if the co-movement did *not* lead the launch, the cross-stream-lag thesis the entire tool rests on would be false. NUKZ (2023 nuclear+AI, before the fund existed) is the **worked proof of a structural claim**, not a lucky anecdote. So the honest question is not "is it possible" — the thesis says it is — but **characterize it**: how much lead time, how much it varies across themes, and the load-bearing one — is the cluster detectable **blind** (no membership handed to you), or only obvious once you already know who ended up in the basket. Those are different questions, and only the blind one is a product.

## 4. The instrument already exists — the validation program

The tool to characterize it is one already built and validated: the **β-strip** (§3.2, proven to remove the common market factor) plus the **dispersion Q** (§2.4), pointed at a *constructed* basket and run **backward** in time. The launch date is the timestamp; the onset of the basket's excess residual-correlation departure is T0; the gap between them is the lead. Run it over a set of themes and you get a *distribution* of leads — the thing a product claim rests on. It is a **program, not a lookup**, because of three traps.

## 5. The three traps — how you fool yourself

1. **Survivorship / look-ahead — the big one (and §1.9/§2.9 already flag it).** Taking an ETF's *launch* holdings and correlating them backward uses knowledge of who *ended up* in the basket. The names that co-moved and then got acquired or delisted before launch are not in your list, and hand-picking a coherent set inflates the correlation you measure. So a retrospective probe answers "*was* the co-movement there?" (cheap, hindsight-assisted — a fine first look) but not "could you have *found* it blind?" (the product claim — unsupervised clustering on a survivorship-clean universe, no membership given). Only the second validates anything sellable.
2. **Market-factor leakage.** Any basket co-moves in a rally. Measure **excess** co-movement (β stripped), never raw — exactly what §3.2 established for the pair sensor, one level down.
3. **Base rate / control.** A hindsight-picked coherent basket almost always shows rising correlation. You need a **control** — random same-sector baskets of the same size — to show the theme basket's excess co-movement beats chance. Without it you see clusters everywhere.

These three are the concrete face of the single safety question in §7: the false-discovery rate.

## 6. Prior art — what exists, and where the white space is

Four layers, three of them mature.

- **Layer 1 — finding price-correlation clusters that cross sector lines.** Foundational and decades deep (Mantegna's correlation trees through a large network-science literature). The recurring result *is* the §1.9 premise: price-derived groupings differ from the standard sectors, and a stock can be influenced by sectors outside its own. Standard method: community detection with a modularity quality index Q — literally the dispersion Q — with significance established by permutation against a **null model**. That is the base-rate control (trap 3) as standard practice, not a gap to invent.
- **Layer 2 — detecting a cluster *forming* / reconfiguring over time.** Active, and where the useful tools live: dynamic/temporal community detection, and change-point detection on correlation matrices (online CUSUM on a Riemannian manifold; the `ruptures` library; a recent entropy-based indicator built specifically to catch cluster-membership reconfiguration — which is exactly what a new theme coalescing *is*). Cleanest existence-proof that emergence-forecasting works: a biomedical-literature pipeline (temporal-network + community detection + link prediction) flagged an emerging COVID theme months early (link-prediction AUC 0.87). Same machine, different substrate.
- **Layer 3 — the productized version, and the tell.** Emerging-theme detection is very much a commercial product — but the big shops build it from **text**, not price. BlackRock/iShares (the THRO thematic-rotation fund) seeds from existing sell-side baskets and thematic funds, then runs NLP on news and earnings calls; Bloomberg sells correlation-based regime detection (MAC3). All of it operates **downstream of packaging and discourse** — §1.2's adversely-selected territory. That the best-resourced quant shops went text-first is a real signal: for *identifying* a theme, text is richer and earlier, so price co-movement is more confirmation than discovery.
- **The gap.** The specific framing here — **price-only, unsupervised, cluster-emergence measured as a survivorship-clean lead over the eventual packaging** — is where there is no clean product or paper. Components mature; the assembly and the lead-as-measured-object are the open part (which is what §1.8 already claims). Encouraging read: nearly everything needed exists and is battle-tested, including survivorship handling (rolling out-of-sample validation, Adjusted Rand Index for cluster stability). Sobering read: price-only being later and noisier than text is probably *why* it is unclaimed — it may be the deterministic floor rather than the edge, which is precisely how §1.9 frames it. The price substrate's real advantages: cheap, deterministic, no NLP, and it can catch a covey syncing *before anyone writes about it* — at the cost of more false clusters to filter.

*Key references to chase down:* Mantegna (1999) and the correlation-network / hierarchical-clustering literature (Onnela, Tumminello); modularity Q with null-model permutation significance; dynamic community detection (DynMSA, Adjusted Rand Index); change-point detection on correlation matrices (`ruptures`; RIO-CPD; the 2026 entropy-based reconfiguration indicator); Kritzman & Li "Financial Turbulence" / absorption ratio (already in the §2.5 lineage); the COVID-literature emergence-forecasting proof-of-concept; commercial (iShares THRO, Bloomberg MAC3); academic thematic-NLP (THEME, SETN, SimStock, NLP-on-10-K AI indices); Ben-David, Franzoni, Kim & Moussawi (2023) "Competition for Attention in the ETF Space" (§1.8) plus the flows-drive-thematic-returns critique; and López de Prado (Hierarchical Risk Parity; *Machine Learning for Asset Managers*) as the practitioner bridge.

## 7. The two dangers — and the two things that make it real rather than dangerous

This is the heart, and where both notes converge. Productizing a pattern-finder has two distinct failure modes, each with its own discipline.

**Danger A — productizing kills the value (the restraint paradox).** The thing that makes Prospector *good* is exactly what makes it hard to sell: its value is the **restraint** — the oracle ceiling, the reading-list-not-order wall, the anti-engagement cadence, the novelty≠interest refusal to show you what already excites you. It works because it makes you trade less and look harder. A paid product's incentives run the other way — retention wants engagement, engagement wants more alerts, more alerts wants a lower ceiling, a lower ceiling wants the firehose — and the moment you optimize for what makes people renew, you have rebuilt the consensus-chasing hype engine the tool exists to refuse. **The value evaporates the instant you optimize it for sale.**

**The discipline for A — the analog-radio principle.** Tunability like an *old analog radio*, not a digital settings page. A digital tuner offers "disable safety limits: y/n" and someone always clicks yes; an analog dial has hard stops *milled into the housing* — the knob turns freely across the legitimate band and physically cannot go past the ends, and there is no menu to remove the stops because they are the shape of the thing, not software. The user tunes the **free band** (which baskets, which corroboration keywords, what counts as on- vs off-thesis, where thresholds sit within reason). The user cannot reach the **stops** — the anti-spurious discipline: placebo-before-calibration, the recall-independence check, corroboration-is-a-witness-not-a-judge, multiple-comparisons honesty, the structural inertness of off-thesis dispositions. Those must be milled into the housing (structural, the way `off-thesis-real`'s inertness is milled into the fold), never a setting a user can tune away. **Acceptance test for whether a shippable version is still the real thing: can the user reach the stops? If yes, don't ship.** This matters *more* for a product than for the owner's own copy — the owner holds the anti-fitting discipline in his own head; a stranger does not, so for them it has to live *entirely* in the housing.

**Danger B — a fully-tunable finder manufactures whatever pattern you want.** Viewed adversarially, a fully-tunable pattern-finder is a machine for manufacturing whatever pattern you unconsciously want — turn the thresholds down, widen the baskets, and it will always find *something*, and a human will always tell a story about why the spurious thing is real. So the entire safety question reduces to the **false-discovery rate**.

**The discipline for B — a *measured* FDR, not an anecdote.** "It found the Financials-2020 decoupling" / "it caught NUKZ" is exactly *one true positive* — it says nothing about how often the machine surfaces noise. Proving the FDR is low enough is therefore **not a phase *after* "prove it works" — it *is* the precondition for the business existing**, because a pattern-finder whose spurious rate you cannot characterize is precisely the thing that must not be sold to strangers with money. The honest sequence is the real gate: the corroboration capstone (first live lag — **now done, see §10**) → calibrate the dispersion sensor (the deferred §3b) → run it live-forward long enough to actually *measure* the noise rate.

## 8. Open questions — the part to sit with

- **What is the minimum viable *honest* claim?** "Co-movement was present before packaging" (retrospective, hindsight-assisted) vs "detectable without being told the membership" (blind). The first is easy and nearly true by §1.5; the second is the real product and the hard one. Is there a middle — blind detection with a *generous* false-positive rate, sold as a **watchlist** rather than a signal?
- **What lead-time distribution makes it sellable?** A lead reliably beyond some months across themes is a product; a lead huge for nuclear and negative for the next theme is a demo. The distribution *is* the feature.
- **The curation problem (what the wrapper was really for).** A proto-basket's membership churns as the thesis forms. A commercial proto-ETF product must either **re-decide** membership on a live, mostly-false-positive signal (operationally heavy, the noisy-desk problem) or **freeze** a T0 snapshot (a worse-curated guess than the eventual fund, since early co-movers are rarely the eventual winners). Fractional shares solve *diversification* but not *maintenance* — so how the product keeps the basket honest over time is the genuinely unsolved design question, and it is the thing §1.4's "professional curator continuously updating the thesis" was actually providing.
- **Who is the user, exactly?** Horizon and rules determine whether early single-name exposure is a feature or a footgun. A different user profile might want the **watchlist** (names to research) rather than a **basket** (names to hold) — which sidesteps the curation problem entirely and might be the better product.
- **The false-discovery rate is the product's precision.** How many spurious clusters per real one? That number, not the nuclear success, is what a serious buyer would ask for — the thing §7's discipline B exists to measure, unmeasurable without the survivorship-clean base-rate work.
- **Regulatory / marketing surface.** What can you *claim* without a survivorship-clean backtest behind it — and does the honest claim ("a research watchlist of forming themes," not "a signal that beats the market") change the product you would build?

## 9. Candidate themes to study (varied lead times and legibility)

A spread chosen so the *distribution* of leads is visible, not a single point:

- **Uranium miners** — the 2021 Sprott physical-trust squeeze; synced hard well before the junior-uranium / nuclear funds packaged it.
- **Generative-AI infrastructure** — post-ChatGPT, synced through 2023 ahead of the gen-AI ETF wave; a short, sharp lead.
- **GLP-1 / obesity** — Lilly and Novo running together from mid-2023, roughly a year before the obesity ETFs.
- **Bitcoin miners** — RIOT / CLSK / CORE / CIFR co-moving through 2023–24 before the digital-asset funds; already in your own holdings data, so the cheapest to probe.
- **Quantum** — a recent, violent sync-up run, recent enough to be a near-live test. (Confirm the specifics — that one is at the edge of what is safe to assert from memory.)

## 10. Where it sits, and how to start

Still v2, downstream of a **calibrated** dispersion sensor and **survivorship-clean** data (the gating dependency — §2.9; worse for exactly this layer, since acquired winners vanish from the survivor set precisely when their thesis was real). But it is **not** a from-scratch build: it is the existing β-strip and dispersion Q run backward over constructed baskets, plus a random-basket control, and the **generous dispersion payload** (§2.4) is already banking the substrate a live version would consume.

**Status (updated 2026-07-04).** When these notes were first written, the next move was "the corroboration hook, the capstone that turns the thesis into a measurement." **That capstone has since landed** — the cross-stream lag now runs on *real* sectors (+593 on real TEKX; existence proof, n=1, not a rate). So the frontier moved by exactly one step: the machine has produced its first real number, and the remaining precondition for *this product* is now the **FDR measurement** — which needs the calibrated dispersion sensor (§3b, still deferred) plus enough live-forward time to measure the noise rate. Everything else here is still downstream of that number. This remains a genuinely *different* project from the personal tool: prove the engine finds real patterns *and* learn how often it finds fake ones, for **one** objective, before building the machine that finds them for anyone's.

**Two ways to start looking (whenever, not now):**
- **A proto-cluster backtest harness** — construct a basket, strip β, roll the within-basket residual correlation and dispersion Q, mark the launch date, read the lead, with the random-basket control built in. Runs on real price data on your Mac (a sandbox can't reach market-data feeds). Answers the *retrospective* question cleanly — the honest first step before the blind version.
- **A quick qualitative first-look** — a search pass over one or two themes to eyeball whether the lead is visibly there before quantifying it.

Neither is urgent, and neither commits anything. The blind, survivorship-clean version — the one that would actually back a product claim — is the larger piece of work, and is genuinely the hard problem §1.9 has been pointing at all along.
